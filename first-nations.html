Reconciliation & Clean Energy

First Nations Framework

A replicable model for reclaiming, remediating, and redeveloping E&N corridor land — built with First Nations.

The Framework

The E&N Railway Corridor intersects 14 First Nations territories. EV Island's pilot program is designed to create a framework that any Nation along the route can adapt and replicate.

The Snaw-Naw-As Precedent

In 2023, the Snaw-Naw-As First Nation won a BC Court of Appeal case returning their portion of the E&N corridor — establishing a landmark legal precedent. The First Nation now govern it as reserve land under the Indian Act, retaining full authority over use, leasing and development.

In 2024, the Snaw-Naw-As completed a Phase 1 and Phase 2 Environmental Site Assessment on their segment. The report confirmed that contamination from creosote-treated railway ties requires active remediation.

EV Island offers a model for true reconciliation.

A New Federal Track

Introducing the First Nations Federal NEV Pilot

Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands are already proving that low-cost, low-speed electric vehicles work — on BC roads, under BC's own Motor Vehicle Act. What's missing isn't demand, community will, or a workable operating framework. It's supply: the affordable, purpose-built Neighbourhood EVs the world is already building aren't yet reaching Canadian roads.

EV Island has submitted an application to the federal government requesting a special case that recognizes internationally-proven vehicle certification as an equivalent pathway, alongside the standards Canada and the U.S. already use. It doesn't create a new vehicle category, and it doesn't change a single existing BC rule — it simply opens the door to more affordable, purpose-built vehicles for communities to draw from.

Vehicle admissibility is federal jurisdiction, regardless of whose land a vehicle ends up on — which is exactly why this particular Pilot is being pursued at the federal, not provincial, level.

Citroen Ami

📨

Application Submitted

EV Island has submitted a formal request for a federal Exemption Order that would enable the adoption of European Union L-category light electric vehicle regulations, thereby permitting the legal importation of certified light Electric Vehicles.

🌐

Two Pilots, Working Together

The Municipal BC NZEV Pilot unlocks where Neighbourhood Zero Emission Vehicles can legally operate, while the First Nations Federal NEV Pilot opens the door to importing EU certified light EVs, benefiting both Pilots.

What Gets Built

Development Priorities

🏘️

Member Housing

New residential sites utilizing the latest solar generation, V2H (vehicle-to-home) charging, and clean energy technologies. Housing is a top priority for most Nations along the corridor.

🏛️

Cultural Facilities

Longhouses, gathering spaces, and cultural programming facilities — built with the latest clean energy technologies integrated from the ground up.

🛒

Commercial Spaces

Stores, markets, artisan spaces, eco-tourism facilities, and NEV education and clean energy demonstration centres — enabled by pathway connectivity.

💧

Green Infrastructure

Constructed wetlands, riparian planting, and carbon credit opportunities — especially significant for Nations like Halalt where flood drainage restoration is a core remediation objective.

A Framework Every Nation Can Use

A pilot launched on any one segment creates a replicable framework that every First Nation along the route can adapt. EV Island is committed to supporting that process every step of the way.
