Where This Started

In 2006, while my company was building a seniors housing development in Chemainus on Vancouver Island, I noticed a few of our residents were quietly driving their golf carts between Stone Gate Estates and Mount Brenton Golf Course. Technically, they shouldn't have been — BC had Low Speed Vehicle legislation on the books, but few affordable qualifying cars existed back then, so the Act was rarely used.

That moment planted the seed. On September 24th, 2017, my mechanic & I drove the same route in a vintage Dynasty electric car — fully legal with ICBC coverage under a grandfathered NZEV provision. It proved that clean, low-speed electric transportation was already available for BC roads. The legislation was already in place — it was just waiting for the political will to apply it.

Dave Haberman

Sun City, AZ

This concept isn't new. Sun City, America's first master-planned community was built around golf carts. Del Webb opened Sun City January 1, 1960. In 1962, Time Magazine recognized him as the "father of the low-speed vehicle movement". The Modified Golf Cart has since evolved into the Neighbourhood EV of today.

The Dynasty Electric Car

The Dynasty Electric Car Corp was a short-lived Kelowna-built Neighbourhood Electric Vehicle. In 2001, the Dynasty qualified as a road-legal Low Speed Vehicle in 47 US states, BC and Quebec. It plugs into a standard 120-volt outlet, costs about 2 pennies per km to run, and has a 50-km range — perfect for most daily use.
