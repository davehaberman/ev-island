The Technology

What is a Neighbourhood EV?

Under BC Motor Vehicle Act Regulations Section 24.06 — which uses the term NZEV — Neighbourhood EVs (NEVs) are four-wheeled electric vehicles capable of speeds up to 40 km/hr. They can operate within defined NEV operating zones with a speed limit of 40 km/hr or less.

These are not golf carts — they are purpose-built electric neighbourhood vehicles, low-cost, low-speed, and built for the electric future Canada is already moving toward.

This EV classification has been legal in BC for decades. My 2001 Kelowna-built NEV (#110) was 20 years ahead of its time.

⚡

Low Cost, Zero Emissions

NEVs cost a fraction of a full size vehicle to buy and run, making clean transportation affordable and accessible to way more drivers.

🏗️

No Highway-Grade Construction

A two-way NEV pathway can be built on gravel, concrete, asphalt, or wood — dramatically reducing infrastructure costs.

🌿

Developable Lands

An NEV pathway opens corridor-adjacent land for much-needed housing, community and commercial development.

The Opportunity

A Shovel-Ready Clean Energy Project

🛤️

289 km Corridor

The dormant E&N railway runs from Victoria to Courtenay, through most of the Island's east-coast communities. Rail makes sense where density supports it — Ottawa is now funding a passenger rail study for Victoria West to Langford. For most of the corridor, recent studies found ridership too low to justify reinstatement. A low-speed EV roadway is a far lower-cost alternative.

🤝

14 First Nations

The corridor intersects 14 First Nation territories. A pilot on any segment is led by that Nation, on its own terms — advancing reconciliation and economic development together.

💰

Economic Development

An NEV corridor connects communities and opens adjacent land for housing, local business and eco-tourism.

The Federal Track

Introducing the First Nations Federal NEV Pilot

Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands are already proving that low-cost, low-speed electric vehicles work — on BC roads, under BC's own Motor Vehicle Act. What's missing is supply: the affordable, purpose-built Neighbourhood EVs the world is already building aren't yet reaching Canadian roads. EV Island has submitted a formal application to the federal government to help open that door. Read more about the Federal Pilot →

Where We Start

In 2023, the Snaw-Naw-As First Nation won a BC Court of Appeal case returning their portion of the corridor, establishing a landmark precedent. Their model offers a tested path for other Nations to follow.

  • 2023 Snaw-Naw-As First Nation wins BC Court of Appeal — corridor land returned, landmark precedent set.
  • 2024 Snaw-Naw-As completes Phase 1 & 2 Environmental Site Assessment on their segment. Active remediation of creosote-treated ties required.
  • 2025 EV Island's booth at the Everything Electric Expo in Vancouver connected with a number of government officials, including a representative from the office of Minister Adrian Dix, Ministry of Energy & Climate Solutions.
  • 2026 Pilot program conversations underway with First Nations.
